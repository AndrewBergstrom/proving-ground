What these loops actually reward

The four scored dimensions

Across FAANG and the newer loops, evaluators score roughly four axes: Communication, Problem Solving, Technical Competency, and Testing. Clarifying questions are scored under two of them at once.

Decomposition beats optimization

The FDE decomposition round is graded on how you break down a problem you have never seen, not on landing the optimal answer. Premature solutioning is the most common single reason candidates are rejected.

Ship, then explain

Practical rounds want a running build and a clear walkthrough, scored on customer framing, build quality, adaptability, and explanation. A working, well-explained solution beats a clever, silent one.

Flagship trainer

Decomposition under ambiguity

The highest-ROI skill in these loops. Read the vague prompt, and before you reveal anything, write down every clarifying question you would ask. Then check yourself against the dimensions strong candidates cover. The goal is reflex: never touch a solution until the problem is pinned down.

Spaced-repetition flashcards

Pattern recognition deck

Pattern recognition is the transferable skill: read the tell, name the pattern. Each card shows a problem's signature. Recall the pattern, flip, then grade yourself. Cards you miss come back sooner. Your schedule is saved on this device.

Evaluation: the part candidates skip

Score retrieval and generation separately

When an answer is wrong, your first question is: bad retrieval or bad generation? Evaluate the two stages independently so you can localize the failure instead of guessing.

Know the metrics

Retrieval: Precision@k, Recall@k, Hit Rate, NDCG@k. Generation: faithfulness / groundedness, often via an LLM-as-judge, which reaches near-human agreement on chunk-level relevance.

Why this app is built the way it is

The study method

This tool encodes what learning science actually says works, from primary sources (Bjork & Bjork's desirable difficulties, the spacing and retrieval-practice literature). The uncomfortable truth: the methods that feel efficient are the ones that fail you under pressure.

01

Retrieval over rereading

Recalling an answer from memory strengthens it far more than reviewing it. That is why the pattern deck makes you name the pattern before it flips, and decomposition makes you write questions before revealing them.

02

Spacing over cramming

Massed practice produces an illusion of mastery that evaporates within a week. Missed cards resurface sooner, mastered cards later. There is no perfect interval; any spaced review beats a single grind.

03

Interleaving over blocking

The deck mixes patterns rather than drilling one at a time. Interleaving forces you to discriminate between confusable patterns, which is exactly what a real, unlabeled interview problem demands.

04

Fading the scaffolding

Worked examples with steps gradually removed beat solved-then-solve pairs. Decomposition shows the dimensions only after you have generated your own, so the support fades as your reflex grows.
